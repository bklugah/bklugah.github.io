About

Profile

I lead the BrainInformatics and Reward Systems (BIRS) Research Group within the Brain Connectivity Lab at the School of Life Science and Technology, UESTC.

My background is interdisciplinary by design — computer science, then mathematical algorithm formulation, then biomedical engineering — and that path shapes how I approach the brain: as a system whose organisation can be measured, modelled and compared across conditions. My doctoral work characterised dynamic functional network connectivity in frontal lobe epilepsy, and the questions have since widened to Reward-based disorders including but not limited to Addiction (behavioral and Substance), affective and reward-related dysfunction(Anhedonia) and affective (mood) disorders(e.g., Major depressive disorder (MDD)).

Methodologically I work with functional and structural MRI, graph-theoretical and network models, multivariate and machine-learning analyses(AI), and coordinate-based meta-analysis. A recurring theme is evidence synthesis: pooling across many small, heterogeneous studies to find the effects that survive. Alongside this I develop software applications for neuroscience and brain health — hub-analysis measures such as MQGA and MIEHS, classification pipelines, and reproducible analysis frameworks that other groups can pick up and run.

I am an Associate Editor at Frontiers in Neuroimaging, a member of the China–Cuba Belt and Road Joint Laboratory on Neurotechnology and Brain-Apparatus Communication at the School of Life Science and Technology, and I supervise doctoral and master's students across computational neuroscience, biomedical engineering and machine learning.
